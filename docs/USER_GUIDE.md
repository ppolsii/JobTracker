## 1. Introduction

### What JobTracker Insights is

JobTracker Insights is a personal job-search command center. It replaces the spreadsheet, the notes app, and the mental to-do list you'd otherwise juggle while looking for a job, and turns everything you do — every application, every stage change, every interview — into a single, organized, searchable history.

### Who it is for

- Anyone actively applying to jobs, whether it's your first search or your fifth.
- People applying with more than one CV (e.g. a "Backend" version and a "Full Stack" version) who want to know which one actually performs better.
- People who want to *know*, not guess, where their search is working and where it isn't.

### What problems it solves

A job search generates a surprising amount of information: which company, which role, which CV you sent, what stage you're at, what a recruiter said last Tuesday, how the technical interview went. Without a system, that information lives in your inbox, your memory, and a dozen browser tabs — and it disappears the moment you forget it.

JobTracker Insights solves this by giving every piece of that information a permanent, structured home: one record per application, one running history per status change, one place for notes, one place for interview feedback.

### Before you sign in: the public website

If you're reading this before creating an account, the public marketing site (starting at the homepage) has everything you need to decide whether JobTracker Insights is right for you: **Features** explains every feature (including which ones are Free vs. Pro), **Pricing** compares the two plans side by side, and **About**/**Contact** cover who it's for and how to reach us. **Privacy Policy** and **Terms of Service** are there too, for the legal details. None of that content requires an account - everything in the rest of this guide, from here on, assumes you're signed in.

### Main philosophy of the application

Everything in JobTracker Insights is built around one idea: **your own history is your best data source.**

- Every number you see (response rates, interview rates, monthly trends) is calculated directly from applications and stages *you* recorded — never estimated, never predicted, never generated by AI.
- Nothing is ever silently deleted. Archiving hides something you're done with; it never erases it. Your full history stays intact for as long as you use the product.
- The more consistently you use it, the more useful it becomes — a single application tells you nothing; fifty applications, tracked consistently, tell you exactly what's working.

## 2. Getting Started

The application is organized around three building blocks: **Companies**, **CV Versions**, and **Applications** — but creating an application is the whole point. Companies are a supporting resource you create *inline, from the application form itself*, never a prerequisite page you have to visit first. CV versions are the one exception: selecting an existing CV and uploading a new one are different actions, so uploading your CV(s) on the **My CVs** page is the only setup step before your very first application.

### Recommended order

1. **Upload at least one CV** on the **My CVs** page (a real PDF) — a one-time step.
2. Click **+ New Application** (on the Dashboard or the Applications page).
3. Choose **Import from job URL** (Pro — pastes and pre-fills from a posting) or **Create manually**.
4. Type the company's name — if it doesn't exist yet, choose **Create "…"** right there in the same field.
5. Choose the CV version you used, fill in the rest, and save.

The Companies and CV Versions pages still exist for management (editing details, viewing analytics, archiving) — Companies is never required first, and CV Versions only ever needs a visit once you actually have a CV to upload.

### Your very first session

A good first session looks like this:

1. Upload your CV on the **My CVs** page, if you haven't already.
2. Click **+ New Application** for the job you most recently applied to (or are about to apply to), creating its company inline as you go.
3. Fill in the position, the application date, and any optional details you know (location, work mode, employment type, source, salary).
4. Repeat for every application you currently have open. Don't worry about applications you've already heard back from — enter them too, with their current status, so your history is complete from day one.

Once you have a handful of applications logged, visit the **Dashboard** and **Analytics** pages — you'll immediately see your first KPIs, even with a small amount of data.

## 3. Complete Job Search Workflow

JobTracker Insights is designed around the natural lifecycle of a single job application. Here is the recommended path from the moment you find a posting to the moment the application is finished with:

```
Finding a job posting
        ↓
Creating the application
        ↓
Tracking status changes
        ↓
Managing interviews
        ↓
Recording interview feedback
        ↓
Receiving an offer or rejection
        ↓
Archiving finished applications
```

### Step 1 — Finding a job posting

This step happens outside the application (on LinkedIn, a job board, a company's careers page, through a referral, etc.). The one thing worth doing at this stage is deciding **which CV version** you'll use to apply — this decision becomes data the moment you log the application.

### Step 2 — Creating the application

As soon as you apply, log it. This is the single most important habit in the entire workflow (see [Best Practices](#14-best-practices)) — an application logged the same day is accurate; an application logged from memory two weeks later is a guess.

### Step 3 — Tracking status changes

Every time something happens — a recruiter reaches out, you're invited to an interview, you're rejected — update the application's status. This is what turns a static list into a living history, and it's what every rate and funnel metric in Analytics is built from.

### Step 4 — Managing interviews

As the application progresses through interview stages (Recruiter Contact, HR Interview, Technical Interview, Final Interview), each stage becomes its own entry in the application's Status History.

### Step 5 — Recording interview feedback

Right after each interview, attach feedback to that specific stage: how it went, what was asked, what you'd do differently. This is the part of the system that helps *you* improve, interview over interview.

### Step 6 — Receiving an offer or rejection

Move the application to **Offer** or **Rejected** as soon as you know. Both are valid, useful outcomes to record — a rejection is still information about what worked and what didn't.

### Step 7 — Archiving finished applications

Once an application reaches its natural end (you accepted, you were rejected, or you're no longer pursuing it), archive it. This keeps your active list focused on what still needs your attention, while preserving the record permanently for your history and analytics.

## 4. Managing Applications

The Applications page is where you spend most of your time. Each application tracks a single position at a single company, using a single CV version.

### Creating an application

Click **+ New Application** (Applications page or Dashboard) and choose **Import from job URL** or **Create manually** — both land you on the same form either way.

Required: company, position, CV version, application date.
Optional but valuable: location, job URL, work mode, employment type, salary, and source (LinkedIn, Indeed, Referral, Company Website, Recruiter, Other).

**Fill in the optional fields whenever you can.** They cost a few extra seconds and directly power some of the most useful Analytics breakdowns (Source Analytics, Work Mode Analytics, Employment Type Analytics).

**Company is never a separate step.** The Company field is a combobox: start typing, pick an existing match, or choose **Create "…"** if it doesn't exist yet — it's created immediately, without leaving the form.

**CV version is a plain selection, on purpose.** The CV field only lets you choose from CVs you've already uploaded — selecting a document and creating one are different actions, so there's no inline upload here. If you don't have any CVs yet, the field shows **No CVs available** with an **Open My CVs** link instead of a dropdown; upload your CV there, then come back and finish the application.

### Smart Job Import (Pro)

Instead of filling in the form yourself, click **+ New Application** and choose **Import from job URL**, then paste a job posting URL. The steps are:

1. Paste the URL and click **Continue**.
2. The app detects what kind of posting it is and attempts to read the job details from it.
3. You land on the same application form, pre-filled with whatever it could read.
4. Review and edit anything before creating the application — every field stays fully editable, nothing is locked in.

If the URL isn't from a supported job board yet, or nothing could be read from it, you're not stuck — you'll see a friendly message with a **Continue manually** option that lands you on the exact same editable form, just blank. Either way, you always end up reviewing and confirming before anything is created.

This is a Pro feature. On the Free plan, choosing **Import from job URL** and trying to import shows the upgrade prompt instead.

### Editing an application

You can update any field at any time — correct a typo, add a job URL you didn't have earlier, fill in the salary once you learn it. Editing a field is not the same as changing its status (see [Status History](#7-status-history)) — editing corrects information, changing status advances the application.

### Searching and filtering

The Applications list shows **Search**, **Status**, and **Company** by default. Click **More filters** to reveal the rest: CV version, source, work mode, employment type, date range, salary range, and sort order — if any of these is already active (e.g. from a link you followed), the panel opens automatically so you never lose track of an applied filter.

Filters are combinable — for example, filter to `Status = Technical Interview` and `Source = Referral` at the same time to see exactly how referral applications are performing at that stage.

### Archiving

Archiving removes an application from your active list without deleting it. Use it once an application is finished (accepted, rejected, or withdrawn) — see [Best Practices](#14-best-practices) for when exactly to do this.

### Restoring

Archived applications aren't gone. Switch to the **Archived** view (from the Applications page) to see everything you've archived, and restore anything you archived by mistake, or that has become relevant again (e.g. a company re-opens contact months later).

### Best practices for managing applications

- Log the application the same day you apply — not at the end of the week.
- Treat "duplicate applications" as normal, not an error: you're allowed to apply to the same company more than once (for a different role, or a second attempt later), and each one deserves its own record.
- Don't leave an application in a stale status. If you haven't heard back in a while, that's still useful information — you don't need to change anything, but don't guess and mark it further along than it actually is.
- If you're on the Free plan, note that it supports up to **15 active (non-archived) applications** at a time. Archiving a finished application frees a slot immediately — one more reason to archive promptly rather than let old, finished applications pile up. Your **Settings** page shows a live usage indicator (e.g. "8 / 15") so you always know how much room you have left, with a gentle heads-up once you're close — see [Plans & Billing](#18-plans--billing).

## 5. Managing Companies

Every application belongs to a company — but you'll normally never visit this page to create one. The Companies page is for managing companies you've already created inline (editing details, browsing, archiving); it's not a required first step.

### When to create a company

Type the company's name into the Company field while creating an application, and choose **Create "…"** if it doesn't already exist — it's created on the spot, with just its name. Come back to this page later if you want to fill in its website, industry, size, or location.

### Keeping company data clean

A company record can hold its website, industry, size, and location, in addition to its name. None of this is required, but filling it in makes your own Company Analytics more meaningful later — a "size" and "industry" field is worth almost nothing on its own, but across dozens of applications it starts to show you, at a glance, which kind of company is responding to you.

### Avoiding duplicates

Company names must be unique per account (matching is case-insensitive, so "Acme" and "acme" are treated as the same company). This is intentional — it keeps every application against a given company grouped together correctly in Company Analytics rather than being silently split across "Acme" and "ACME" as if they were two different employers.

If you archive a company by mistake and then try to re-create it with the same name, restore the original instead — this keeps its full application history attached to one record.

## 6. Managing CV Versions

### Why CV versions matter

If you apply with more than one version of your CV (a "Backend Engineer" version and a "Full Stack" version, for example, or an older CV and a recently updated one), JobTracker Insights lets you track each one as its own CV Version, and attach every application to the one you actually used.

This is what turns "I think my new CV is doing better" into a measurable fact.

### Uploading, downloading, and replacing a CV

Each CV version holds a real PDF file (up to 5 MB). **All CV document management — uploading, editing, replacing, downloading — happens on the My CVs page only**; the application form never opens an upload dialog, since selecting a CV and creating one are different actions. From the CV Versions page you can **Download** a CV at any time, or **Edit** to replace its PDF (its name/description can also be updated without touching the file). CV versions created before this feature existed don't have a file yet; edit one to upload its file for the first time.

### Deleting a CV version

Click **Delete** on a CV version that's no longer in use — it's removed from your CV Versions list and any picker immediately (this codebase never truly erases data; the same recoverable archiving every other list in this app uses). If a CV version is still referenced by one or more applications, deleting it isn't allowed — you'll see exactly how many applications use it instead of a generic error. Reassign or remove those applications first (or simply keep using the CV version) if you want it gone from your active list.

### Recommended naming conventions

Name each CV version for what makes it *different*, not for its file name or date:

| Good | Why |
|---|---|
| `Backend Engineer` | Describes the target role. |
| `Full Stack (2026)` | Describes the focus and the version. |
| `Senior — Leadership Focus` | Describes the angle, not just the seniority. |

Avoid names like `CV_final_v3` or `Resume2` — they're meaningless once you're comparing performance six months from now.

### How CV versions help analytics

CV Analytics ranks your CV versions by interview rate, so the version that actually gets you further shows up clearly — but only once a CV version has enough applications behind it (10 or more) to be a meaningful comparison rather than noise. Until then, that CV will simply show as not having enough data yet, which is expected and not a problem — keep using it consistently and the picture will fill in.

## 7. Status History

Every application has a Status History: a permanent, chronological log of every stage it has passed through, from the moment it was created to wherever it stands today.

### Why it exists

Status History is what separates "I think I got a lot of interviews" from an actual, provable number. It's the single source of truth behind every rate, every funnel stage, and every time-based metric (like Average Response Time) in the entire application.

### When to change status

Change an application's status the moment something actually happens — not in batches, and not from memory later. Typical triggers:

- You submit the application → move it from **Wishlist** to **Applied**.
- A recruiter reaches out → **Recruiter Contact**.
- You're invited to a stage → **HR Interview**, **Technical Interview**, or **Final Interview**, in order.
- You receive an offer → **Offer**.
- You accept → **Accepted**. You're turned down at any point → **Rejected**.

Status changes only move forward — you can't skip a stage or go backwards, which keeps the history honest and consistent.

### Why chronological tracking matters

Status History is append-only: once a stage is recorded, it's never edited or removed, even later. This matters for two reasons:

1. **Trust** — you can always look back and see exactly what happened and when, with no risk of a later edit quietly rewriting your own history.
2. **Accuracy** — every timing metric (how long it took to hear back, how long it took to get an offer) depends on these timestamps being real and untouched.

## 8. Interview Feedback

Once an application has at least one status change into an interview stage, you can attach structured feedback directly to that specific stage.

### When to create feedback

As soon as possible after the interview — ideally the same day, while the details are still fresh. You can add more than one feedback entry to the same stage (useful if you were interviewed by two different people in the same round).

### How to use ratings

Each feedback entry can include an optional 1–5 rating and a format (Phone, Video, On-site, Technical, or Behavioral). Use the rating as your own honest sense of how it went, not how you think it will turn out — a 2/5 technical interview you know you fumbled is exactly the record you'll want to revisit before your next one.

### How to write useful notes

Good interview notes are specific and reusable. Aim to capture:

- What was actually asked (questions, exercises, topics).
- How you answered, and where you hesitated.
- Anything about the interviewer or format worth remembering.
- What you'd do differently next time.

| Less useful | More useful |
|---|---|
| "It went okay." | "Two live coding questions on arrays/strings, 45 min. Struggled to explain time complexity out loud — practice that." |
| "Bad interview." | "Behavioral round focused entirely on conflict resolution. I didn't have a strong example ready — prepare one before the next final round." |

### How feedback helps improve future interviews

Interview Feedback is your own preparation log. Before your next Technical Interview or Final Interview — especially at a similar type of company — reread your past feedback for that same stage. Patterns jump out quickly: the same kind of question keeps coming up, or the same moment keeps tripping you up. That's exactly what you want to walk into the next interview already knowing.

## 9. Analytics

The Analytics page is where your history turns into insight. Every number on this page is calculated directly from your own applications and status history — nothing here is estimated or predicted. Metrics that don't yet have enough data to be meaningful are clearly marked as such rather than shown as a misleading number.

### Top-level rates

| Metric | What it means | How to interpret it | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Response Rate** | Share of applications that got any reply at all (anything past "Applied"). | Low response rate usually points to the application materials or targeting, not the interview process. | If it's low, revisit your CV version or how you're sourcing roles. |
| **Interview Rate** | Share of applications that reached at least one interview stage. | Reflects how well your profile matches what you're applying to. | If Response Rate is fine but Interview Rate is low, your CV may be getting opened but not convincing. |
| **Offer Rate** | Share of applications that reached an offer. | Reflects your overall conversion from application to offer. | Track this over time as your overall benchmark. |
| **Acceptance Rate** | Of the offers you received, how many you accepted. | This is about your own decision-making, not performance. | Not something to "improve" — just a useful record of your own choices. |

### Time metrics

| Metric | What it means | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Average Response Time** | How long, on average, it takes to hear back after applying. | Helps you calibrate patience — if your average is three weeks, don't panic at day five. |
| **Average Offer Time** | How long, on average, it takes to go from applying to receiving an offer. | Useful for planning — if you have a deadline (e.g. a start date), this tells you how far in advance to start applying. |
| **Average Hiring Time** | How long, on average, it takes from applying to being accepted. | The full, real timeline of your search, end to end. |

### Insights

A short list of plain-language observations generated directly from your data — for example, which CV performs best, which source gets you the most interviews, or which stage of the funnel you tend to drop out of. Insights only appear once there's enough data behind them; an empty or short list simply means you don't have enough history yet for a fair comparison.

### Trend Analysis

Compares your current month against the previous one — application volume, interviews, offers, and responses, each shown as a percentage change. Use it as a monthly check-in: a sudden drop in applications this month, for instance, is far more visible here than it would be scrolling through a list.

### Funnel

Shows exactly how many applications entered each stage (Applied → Recruiter Contact → HR Interview → Technical Interview → Final Interview → Offer → Accepted), how many progressed to the next one, and how many were lost at each point. This is the fastest way to answer "where do I actually lose most of my applications?"

### Monthly, Company, CV, and Source Analytics

Four tables, each breaking down your core numbers (applications, responses, interviews, offers, rates, average response time) by a different dimension:

- **Monthly Analytics** — your activity and results over time.
- **Company Analytics** — which companies you hear back from, sortable by any column.
- **CV Analytics** — which CV version performs best, sorted by interview rate by default.
- **Source Analytics** — which channel (referral, job board, recruiter, etc.) is actually working.

### Work Mode and Employment Type Analytics

Two more focused breakdowns: how you perform across Remote/Hybrid/On-site roles, and across Full Time/Part Time/Internship/Contract/Freelance roles. Useful if you're applying broadly across more than one of these and want to know if one is clearly working better than another.

### Interpreting "not enough data" messages

Several metrics only appear once you cross a minimum amount of history (for example, comparing CV versions fairly needs a reasonable number of applications behind each one, and month-over-month trends need at least two months of activity). Seeing "not enough historical data" isn't an error — it's the application refusing to show you a comparison that wouldn't be fair yet. Keep logging consistently and it will resolve itself.

### Advanced Analytics (Pro)

Click **Advanced Analytics** at the top of the Analytics page for a deeper, filterable dashboard built for actually changing how you search, not just tracking it. It sits alongside the Analytics page described above — nothing here replaces it, and everything above stays free.

**Filter everything at once.** Advanced Analytics has its own filter bar — date range, company, CV version, work mode, employment type, status — and every section below reacts to whatever you filter to. Want to know how your Remote applications specifically have performed since March? Set both filters and every section updates together.

What you'll find, section by section:

| Section | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Application Funnel** | The same stage-by-stage conversion as the base Funnel, recalculated for your current filter. |
| **Company Performance** | Your top and worst companies by interview rate, plus your average response time across all of them — not just a sortable table, an actual ranking. |
| **CV Performance** | Every CV version compared side by side, filtered to whatever you've selected. |
| **Timeline Analysis** | The average number of days between each stage of your process (Application → Recruiter Contact → Interview → Offer → Accepted), plus your fastest, slowest, and typical (median) time from application to acceptance. |
| **Job Search Activity** | Your current and longest daily application streaks, your most and least active months, and a recent-weeks activity chart. |
| **Work Modality** and **Employment Type** | The same comparisons from the base Analytics page, but filterable here. |
| **Salary** | Minimum, maximum, average, and median salary across your applications, a distribution of ranges, and average salary broken down by company and by work mode — only shown once you've recorded at least some salary data. |
| **Personal Insights** | Deterministic observations specific to Advanced Analytics (for example, which work modality gets you more interviews, or which day of the week you tend to apply on most successfully) — shown only once there's enough data to say so honestly. |

This is a Pro feature. On the Free plan, clicking **Advanced Analytics** shows the upgrade prompt instead.

## 10. Calendar (Pro)

**Calendar**, in the main navigation, gives you one visual view of every important moment across your entire job search — every application submitted, every recruiter reply, every interview, every offer — instead of piecing that together from separate application pages.

### Views

- **Month view** (the default on desktop) shows a full month at a glance, with up to three events per day and a "+N more" link for busier days.
- **Week view** shows one week with full event titles.
- **Agenda view** (the default on mobile) is a flat, scrollable list of events grouped by date — the easiest way to read a lot of events on a small screen.

Switch between the three at any time using the view switcher above the calendar.

### Event types

Every event on the calendar is one of these, each with its own icon and color so you can tell them apart at a glance (hover any event for a tooltip naming its type):

| Type | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| Application Submitted | Automatically, the moment you log an application |
| Recruiter Contact | Automatically, when you record that stage change |
| Interview / Technical Interview / Final Interview | Automatically, from your status history |
| Offer Received / Offer Accepted / Offer Rejected | Automatically, from your status history |
| Rejected | Automatically, for a rejection at any earlier stage |
| Reminder | Created by you |
| Custom Event | Created by you |

The automatic ones are never edited or deleted from the Calendar directly — they're a read-only reflection of your real Status History, which you already manage from the application itself.

### Event details

Click any event to open its details in a side panel: company, position, date, current status, description, any notes on the application, and — for events tied to an application — that application's full timeline (with Interview Feedback shown inline wherever you recorded it, exactly as it appears on the application's own page). From there you can jump straight to **Open Application**, or, for a Reminder/Custom Event you created, **Edit Event**.

### Creating a Reminder or Custom Event

Use **New Event** to add something the automatic history doesn't capture — a reminder to follow up, a networking coffee, a deadline you want visible on the calendar. Give it a type (Reminder or Custom Event), a title, a date, an optional description, and optionally link it to one of your applications so it shows up in that application's context too.

### Filters and search

The Calendar has its own filter bar — company, event type, application status, date range, work mode, employment type — and a search box that matches company name, position, and note content, the same way [Search](#13-search) does elsewhere in the app. Filters and search combine: search only looks within whatever the filters have already narrowed down to.

This is a Pro feature. On the Free plan, clicking **Calendar** shows the upgrade prompt instead.

## 11. Goals, Achievements & Progress

**Goals**, in the main navigation, turns your job search into something you can actively work toward instead of just record — set targets, watch your progress, and get recognized for staying consistent.

### Setting a goal

Click **Create goal** and choose:

- **Metric** — Applications, Interviews, Offers, or Recruiter Contacts.
- **Period** — Weekly, Monthly, Quarterly, Yearly, or Total (a single, all-time target that never resets — e.g. "100 applications total").
- **Target** — how many you're aiming for in that period.

A weekly/monthly/quarterly/yearly goal recurs automatically — once its period ends, a new one starts, and your progress resets for the new period. A Total goal never resets; it accumulates for as long as the goal exists.

### Tracking progress

Every goal card shows its current value, target, remaining amount, percentage complete, and — while it isn't finished yet — an estimated completion date based on your current pace. A goal that's been reached is marked **Achieved**.

### Managing goals

Each goal can be **Edited** (change its metric, period, or target), **Paused** (temporarily excluded without losing it), **Reactivated**, **Archived** (moved out of your active list, but kept for reference), or **Deleted** (removed permanently — your applications and their history are never affected either way).

### Streaks

Three streaks are tracked automatically from your application history: a **daily** streak (consecutive days with at least one application), a **weekly** streak (consecutive weeks with at least one application), and a **monthly** streak (consecutive months with at least one application). Each shows both its current run and your longest ever.

### Achievements

Achievements unlock automatically as you go — nothing to claim or configure. They include application milestones (1, 10, 50, 100), first-time milestones (First Interview, First Offer, First Accepted Offer), and consistency-based ones (Interview Master, Offer Hunter, Consistent Week, 30-Day Streak). The Achievements grid shows every achievement, locked or not, so you can see what's still ahead.

### Statistics

The top of the Goals page summarizes: completed goals, active goals, completion rate, average time to completion, longest streak, and current streak.

### On your Dashboard

A compact Goals widget on the Dashboard shows today's application count, your active weekly goals' progress, your most recently unlocked achievements, and upcoming goal deadlines — so you don't need to visit the Goals page just to check in.

### On your Calendar

Every active, recurring goal shows its current period's deadline on the [Calendar](#10-calendar) too, so you see it alongside everything else in your search. A goal already met by its deadline appears visually distinct (a checkmark, in emerald) from one still in progress.

This is a Pro feature. On the Free plan, clicking **Goals** shows the upgrade prompt instead.

## 12. Notification Center & Smart Reminders

The bell icon in the top navigation opens your **Notification Center** — one place that proactively surfaces what needs your attention, instead of you having to check every page yourself. A red badge on the bell shows how many notifications are unread.

### Where notifications come from

Every notification is generated automatically from your own real data — nothing is invented. The Notification Center reuses the exact same Calendar, Goals, and Achievements information you already see elsewhere:

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Application** | "Application becoming stale," "No activity for N days," "Follow-up recommended" — for an open application with no recent status change (e.g. "You applied to Roche 12 days ago"). |
| **Interview** | "Interview today" / "Interview tomorrow" — when an interview-stage event falls on either date. |
| **Goal** | "Weekly goal completed," "Goal behind schedule," "Goal deadline approaching," "Almost there!" (close to your target) — read straight off your Goals page's own progress. |
| **Achievement** | "Achievement unlocked" — the moment a new achievement is earned. |
| **Calendar** | "Today's event" / "Upcoming event" — for anything else on your Calendar within the next week. |
| **General** | A one-time welcome message for new accounts, and the occasional tip (e.g. a nudge to set your first goal). |

### Grouping

Notifications are grouped by **Today**, **Yesterday**, **This Week**, and **Older**, so recent, relevant things stay at the top.

### Managing notifications

Each notification shows an icon, title, description, timestamp, and (where relevant) a button to jump straight to the application, Calendar, or Goals page it's about. You can **mark it as read**, **archive** it, or **delete** it individually — or use **Mark all as read** / **Delete all read** at the top of the page for bulk cleanup.

### Filtering and search

Filter by unread only, category, company, or a date range, and search by title, description, or company name — the same combinable-filters pattern used throughout the app.

### On your Dashboard

A compact Notifications widget shows your latest few notifications, your unread count, and a quick "Mark all as read" action.

This is a Pro feature. On the Free plan, clicking the bell (or visiting Notifications directly) shows the upgrade prompt instead.

## 13. Search

JobTracker Insights gives you two ways to search, for two different needs.

### Global search

Available from every page, in the top navigation. Type a few characters and get a quick, capped set of matching Companies, Applications, and Notes instantly — built for "I just need to jump to that one application," not for browsing.

### Dedicated Search page

Reached from the global search dropdown's "View all results" link. This is the full version: three independent sections (Companies, Applications, Notes), each with its own result count and its own pagination, so a long list of matching notes doesn't push matching applications off the page.

### Searching companies

Matches on company name, case-insensitive, partial matches included — searching "acm" will find "Acme Inc."

### Searching notes

Matches on note content. This is especially useful months into a search, when you vaguely remember writing something down ("I know I wrote a note about a weird interview question…") but don't remember which application it was on.

## 14. Best Practices

A short checklist of habits that make the biggest difference:

- [ ] Create the application the same day you apply — not from memory later.
- [ ] Update the status as soon as anything changes — don't batch it up.
- [ ] Record interview feedback right after the interview, while it's fresh.
- [ ] Keep company names consistent — don't create "Acme" and "Acme Inc." for the same employer.
- [ ] Fill in optional fields (source, work mode, employment type, salary) whenever you know them — Analytics is only as good as the data behind it.
- [ ] Review Analytics at least once a week.
- [ ] Archive applications as soon as they're finished (accepted, rejected, or withdrawn) — keep your active list to what still needs attention.
- [ ] Use the Archived view to restore anything closed too early, rather than re-creating it from scratch.
- [ ] Give each CV version a name that describes what it *is*, not when you saved it.
- [ ] Write interview notes as if you'll read them again in three months — because you will.

A simple routine for someone actively job hunting:

## 16. Tips for Getting the Most Value

JobTracker Insights is a compounding tool: the value it gives you grows with the consistency you put into it, not with how long you've had it installed.

- **A handful of applications tells you almost nothing.** Response rates, CV comparisons, and source comparisons only become meaningful once you have a real sample size behind them — that's *by design*, so the app never shows you a misleading conclusion from too little data.
- **Gaps hurt more than you'd think.** A week where you applied but didn't log anything creates a blind spot in your own Monthly Analytics and Trend Analysis later — there's no way to reconstruct it after the fact.
- **Revisit your CV comparison periodically.** As each CV version accumulates more applications, its comparison against your other versions gets more reliable — check back on it every few weeks rather than drawing a conclusion from your first ten applications.
- **Treat rejections as data, not just disappointments.** A rejected application is still a complete, useful record — it's what makes your Response Rate, Interview Rate, and Funnel numbers real instead of only reflecting your best outcomes.
- **Let the Insights section surprise you.** It's built specifically to surface things you might not notice yourself while you're in the middle of the search.

## 17. Frequently Asked Questions

**When should I archive an application?**
As soon as it reaches a real end point — you accepted an offer, you were rejected, or you've decided not to pursue it further. Archiving doesn't delete anything; it just moves it out of your active list while keeping its full history intact for your records and your analytics.

**What if I applied twice to the same company?**
That's expected and fully supported — applying more than once to the same company (for a different role, or trying again later) is common, and each attempt gets its own application record with its own status history.

**Should I create multiple CV versions?**
Yes, if you actually use more than one version of your CV. The point isn't to create versions for their own sake — it's to make sure every application is linked to the CV you genuinely sent, so CV Analytics reflects reality.

**How should I rate an interview?**
Rate it honestly, based on how you think it actually went — not how you hope it turns out. A low rating paired with a specific note about what went wrong is far more useful to your future self than an optimistic rating with no detail.

**I archived something by mistake — is it gone?**
No. Open the Archived view for that section (Applications, Companies, or CV Versions) and restore it — it comes back exactly as it was, with its history untouched.

**Can I change an application's status by mistake?**
Status changes only move forward along the allowed path (you can't skip stages or go backwards), which prevents most accidental mistakes. If you genuinely recorded the wrong stage, treat it the same way you would a real-world correction — move it forward again once the actual, correct event happens, and use the notes on the application to record what actually occurred.

**What happens if I reach the Free plan's application limit?**
The Free plan supports up to 15 active applications at a time. When you try to create a 16th, you'll see a dedicated screen explaining the limit and your options — archive a finished application to free up a slot, or upgrade to Pro for unlimited applications — rather than a plain error message. Archiving any finished application frees a slot immediately, which is another good reason to archive promptly rather than let old, closed-out applications sit in your active list.

**How do I upgrade to Pro, or see what Pro includes?**
Visit the **Pricing** page (in the sidebar) at any time to compare Free and Pro side by side. Your current plan, usage, and an Upgrade button are also always available from **Settings → Subscription**.

**What happens to my data if I cancel Pro or a payment fails?**
Nothing is ever deleted. Cancelling keeps your Pro access until the end of the billing period you already paid for, then moves you to Free - all your applications, companies, CV versions, notes, and history stay exactly as they are. A failed payment doesn't touch your data either; you'll see a clear notice and a grace period to update your payment method through Manage Subscription before anything changes.

**Is my payment information stored by JobTracker Insights?**
No. Checkout and subscription management both happen on Stripe's own secure pages - your card details are never sent to or stored by JobTracker Insights.

**What does Smart Job Import actually do right now?**
Paste a job URL and the app will try to detect the posting and read its details for you automatically. Support for specific job boards is being added over time - until a given site is supported, or if nothing could be read from the page, you'll be dropped straight into the same editable application form with just the URL filled in, so you can complete it yourself in a few seconds either way. Nothing is ever created without you reviewing and confirming it first.

**Does the Calendar's "Reminder" send me a notification?**
A Reminder is still just a calendar event you create to mark something on a date — no email or push notification is sent for it specifically. That said, anything on your Calendar within the next week (Reminders included) does surface as a notification in your [Notification Center](#12-notification-center--smart-reminders), so you'll still see it there even without a separate alert.

**What kinds of things does the Notification Center actually catch?**
Only what's real in your own data: an open application with no recent activity, an interview happening today or tomorrow, goal progress (completed, behind schedule, or close to your target), a newly unlocked achievement, and anything coming up on your Calendar. Nothing is ever fabricated or guessed — every notification traces back to something you already logged.

**What happens to a weekly/monthly goal once its period ends?**
It resets automatically. A new period starts (a new week, month, quarter, or year) and its progress begins again from zero for that period - the goal itself isn't recreated, just its current window. A "Total" goal never resets - it's a single, cumulative target for as long as it exists.

**Do achievements ever get taken away, or need to be claimed?**
No. Achievements unlock automatically the moment you meet their condition, and stay unlocked permanently - there's nothing to claim and nothing that can revoke one later.

**Why don't I see a Custom goal option?**
Custom goals (tracking something other than Applications, Interviews, Offers, or Recruiter Contacts) aren't available yet - the architecture supports adding them in a future update, but this phase ships only the four built-in metrics.

**Why don't I see a Salary section on Advanced Analytics?**
Salary insights only appear once you've recorded a salary range (minimum and/or maximum) on at least one application. It's not hidden or broken - there's simply nothing to summarize yet. Add a salary range to a few applications and it will appear on your next visit.

**Why does a metric say "not enough historical data"?**
Some comparisons (CV performance, month-over-month trends, company comparisons) need a minimum amount of history before they're statistically fair. Rather than show you a misleading number from too small a sample, the application waits until there's enough data, and tells you so plainly in the meantime.

**Can I search for something I wrote in a note months ago?**
Yes — use the dedicated Search page (or the global search in the top navigation) and search by whatever text you remember from the note. Notes are searched by their content, not just their title.

## 18. Plans & Billing

JobTracker Insights has two plans: **Free** and **Pro**. Every feature described elsewhere in this guide — Companies, CV Versions, Status History, Notes, Interview Feedback, Search, and Analytics — is fully available on both. The difference between the two plans is capacity and convenience, not access to your own data.

### Free

- Up to 15 active applications at a time (archived applications don't count against this).
- Companies, CV management, Status History and Timeline, Notes, Interview Feedback, Search, and Analytics — all included, with no feature locked away.

### Pro

- **Unlimited active applications** — never archive early just to make room for a new one.
- **Export your data** to CSV or JSON at any time.
- **Smart Job Import** — create an application by pasting a job posting URL instead of filling in the form yourself (see [Managing Applications](#4-managing-applications)).
- **Advanced Analytics** — a deeper, filterable dashboard: company/CV ranking, timeline analysis, activity streaks, and salary insights (see [Analytics](#9-analytics)).
- **Calendar** — visualize every interview, offer, and reminder across Month/Week/Agenda views (see [Calendar](#10-calendar)).
- **Goals, Achievements & Progress** — set application/interview/offer/recruiter-contact targets, track daily/weekly/monthly streaks, and unlock achievements automatically (see [Goals, Achievements & Progress](#11-goals-achievements--progress)).
- **Notification Center & Smart Reminders** — proactive, centralized notifications about stale applications, upcoming interviews, goal progress, and unlocked achievements (see [Notification Center & Smart Reminders](#12-notification-center--smart-reminders)).

### The Pricing page

Reachable from the sidebar at any time, the Pricing page lays out both plans side by side, plus a full feature-by-feature comparison table — the fastest way to see exactly what upgrading gets you. (If you're browsing before creating an account, the public website's own Pricing page — reached from the homepage — shows the same comparison.)

### Your Subscription section

Open **Settings → Subscription** to see your current plan, status, renewal date, billing interval (Monthly or Yearly), and — on the Free plan — a live usage indicator showing exactly how many of your 15 application slots are in use. This is also where you'll find the Upgrade button, or — once you're on Pro — a button to manage your subscription.

### Upgrading to Pro

Choosing **Upgrade monthly** or **Upgrade yearly** (from the Pricing page or Settings) takes you to Stripe's own secure Checkout page to enter your payment details — nothing about your card ever passes through or is stored by JobTracker Insights itself. Once your payment succeeds, you're redirected back and your account is upgraded automatically, usually within a few seconds. If you close the Checkout page without paying, you're simply returned to Settings with no charge and no change to your plan.

### Managing your subscription

Once you're on Pro, **Manage subscription** (in Settings) takes you to Stripe's Customer Portal, where you can:

- Update your payment method.
- Download past invoices.
- Switch between Monthly and Yearly billing.
- Cancel your subscription (you'll keep Pro access until the end of your current billing period, then automatically move to Free — none of your data is ever affected).
- Resume a subscription you'd previously scheduled to cancel.

### If a payment fails

If a renewal payment doesn't go through, your Settings page will clearly flag it and you'll keep your Pro access for a short grace period while Stripe automatically retries the charge. Use **Manage subscription** at any time during this window to update your payment method and resolve it — you won't lose any data, and once a payment succeeds, the notice clears automatically.

### The Usage Indicator

On the Free plan, your Settings page always shows how many active applications you're using out of your 15-application limit, with a progress bar. Two things change as you get closer to the limit:

- **At 80% or more** (12 of 15 or higher), you'll see a gentle note that you're approaching the limit — a good moment to archive anything finished, or consider Pro if you expect to keep growing your active list.
- **At the limit** (15 of 15), the indicator clearly states you've reached it, with an Upgrade button right there.

### What happens at the limit

If you try to create a 16th active application on the Free plan, you won't just get an error — you'll see a dedicated screen explaining that you've reached the Free plan limit, what unlimited applications and Export look like on Pro, and what's coming next. From there you can choose to upgrade, or simply close the screen and archive something first ("Maybe Later").

### Upgrading a specific feature

Some features (like Export) are Pro-only. If you try to use one on the Free plan, you'll see a clear explanation of why it's part of Pro and what you'd gain by upgrading — never a generic error message.
